# 14M: actual analyses, reconsidered decisions and regulatory approval

Status: accepted as port 28 of 48, with 20 remaining. Five actual Wasm scientists,
four effect-owner localsides and 25 projected programs execute the whole study.
Twelve scripted conditions and one five-model pilot pass common scoring,
dynamic EPF source interpretation and the separate actual-effect audit on their
recorded sources. This is not final all-family certification or a scored comparison.

The business global includes all eleven analyses, result-dependent retries,
fresh decisions after regulatory rejection, optional biology/toxicology/both/no
reanalysis, and approved documentation. Independent analyses use `par`. Actual
instrument Endpoints carry requests; scientific continuation Endpoints pass to
the effect receiver before its real result is known. A failure returns the
continuation for another attempt. Success sends it to the lead. Regulatory
approval transfers the study to the chemist, closing the lead's reanalysis path.
There is no application owner table, phase flag or protocol driver.

The HPLC, cell-laboratory, mass-spectrometry and regulatory receivers write their
Endpoint operations directly. They compare received requests and actual data
before effects. Decision and approval checks bind current artifact receipts;
neither go nor no-go is forced. Actual approval is communicated to participants
waiting for possible reanalysis, giving their localsides a termination boundary.

14E and 14M now use one material/result implementation. Analyses name the actual
compound and completed inputs. Stability may use an incubating prepared medium;
a final decision still needs the completed bioassay. Independent cell preparation,
baseline markers and analytical methods remain independent. Failed or pending
replacement results do not fall back to older positive results.

The lead scientist's actual `submit_decision(go)` is available to every method.
Regulatory review consumes that decision's completed analysis receipts. Rejection
requires a new decision; the world does not prescribe extra assays or one
scientist schedule. New evidence or a new decision invalidates the old approval.
Documentation requires the current decision and its actual completed approval.
Both go and no-go outcomes may be documented.

The common event journal owns copies of request/result JSON. A tool caller cannot
rewrite its scientific evidence by modifying a returned nested object. This is
actual data ownership, with no workflow cursor, owner table or phase flag.

The declared synthetic latency is 1–5 seconds for analyses and review, and zero
for recording a decision, scaled equally for every method. It is a benchmark
latency model rather than measured laboratory performance. Existing run archives
retain their own earlier world versions and are not relabeled as v15 results.
