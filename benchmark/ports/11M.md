# 11M: actual parts, reusable tools and inspected rework

Five scripted wash guests ran 30 trials. Across all six jobs, the workers
manufactured 180 parts and the inspector measured each one. The 48 negative
measurements produced 48 dispatch effects. Each order named the rejected part
and inspection and reached the assigned worker through that worker's ChoreoFS
file. The audit matched 203,355 EPF records across the trials. The simulator
reported zero violations.

The six trials with no injected rejection completed the business score. The
other 24 ended with replacement work outstanding. The next end-to-end check is
to manufacture the replacement on the shared equipment, measure the new part,
and complete the task with five autonomous agents.

### Dispatch and delivery of a rework order

A fifth wash guest receives the inspector's actual verdict through its
dispatcher Endpoint. For a failed part, the dispatcher writes the job and an
optional reason to `/service/dispatch/{job_id}`. The worker holding the
material receives that request and executes it against the failed inspection.
The dispatcher can read the resulting order from the same file. The assigned
worker reads that order through `/service/job_update` on its own workstation.
For an approved part the workstation receives the original inspection verdict.
In all six serial trials the worker read its approval before the dispatcher
opened its copy.

Five scripted guests ran 18 trials on the common simulator across all six
submission orders. Each order ran with zero, one and three failed inspections.
When all three parts failed, the dispatcher issued orders as failures arrived
in three trials and in reverse inspection order in the other three. The same
run's EPF capture, received request, simulator result and shell output agreed
on all 24 orders, including the failed part, inspection receipt, assigned
worker and submitted reason. The shell refused 18 premature requests, 30
requests for approved jobs, 24 wrong-job writes and 24 duplicates. The journals
contained precisely 54 manufacturing, 54 inspection and 24 dispatch effects.
The next manufacturing attempt still needs to reacquire its workstation and
tool before producing a replacement. The six-job business score remained
incomplete in every trial. [Run evidence and hashes](../../analysis/11m-wash-dispatch.json)

### Rework while another worker holds a tool

In six scripted Wasm trials, manufacturing continued on the second job throughout
dispatch of the first job's rework order. The third worker had not yet submitted
its job. Each worker took each position. Both the simulator intervals and the
request/reply journal confirm the overlap. Across all 24 Wasm trials EPF
checked 97,738 records for 174 simulator effects. The application executable
was byte-identical to the preceding dispatch build. These runs stop at delivery
of the order. The test checks attempted repeat writes before dispatch and after
approval, when another manufacturing attempt really is disallowed.

In a separate native test, the cabinet's production tool-0 loan, request,
reply and return are inside the job's inspection and rework continuation.
Another worker holds tool 1 across the entire run. The three tool jobs made
30 requests on tool 0, including 18 replacement parts. Every replacement
matched the actual failed material and completed dispatch. The three jobs
without a tool made zero tool requests. EPF checked 4,248 records across
the 24 job/rejection cases. This fixture uses controlled effects. Connecting
these continuations to the common six-job wash allocator remains open.
[Cases, executable hashes and journal hashes](../../analysis/11m-independent-rework.json)

The version-13 common world derives current parts, quality receipts and rework
orders from its actual effect journal. A replacement has a new part identity;
the old part's positive inspection cannot certify it. An inspection reads the
current completed part. An actual failed inspection requires a new part, not
repeated inspection of the unchanged rejected material. Dispatch returns an
order referring to that completed failed inspection and part. Rework without a
matching completed current order remains a physical action and records a causal
violation. A later repair does not erase it. Early inspections and dispatches
without their real inputs return ordinary unavailable-input results and can be
retried after the inputs exist.

Metadata names each job's actual station and optional cabinet tool. The common
registry observes overlapping calls; it does not wait or schedule callers.
Two specialized jobs on different stations may overlap. A third simultaneous
tool use is a capacity violation; completion or I/O failure returns the tool.
The inspector itself is now named as a capacity-one physical resource, exposing
the task's existing one-inspection-at-a-time condition in the same metadata for
all methods. No new participant is introduced.

All independent job and inspection orders remain free. Work on another part may
overlap inspection or dispatch for this part; those actions do not depend on
each other's data. The world does not require a worker to follow one protocol
or wait for an unrelated inspection. Common completion requires positive,
completed inspections of all six actual current assigned parts; resource and
causal violations are also scored. Arbitrary unsafe calls remain observable.

### Typed requests at the physical effect

In `54c32cb`, the cabinet submits the typed request received from the loaned
tool. That request fixes the worker, job and simulator operation. The native
quality component likewise consumes separate typed receives for manufacturing,
inspection and rework dispatch. A job and an inspection can carry identical
JSON while authorizing different effects.

All six cabinet arrival orders passed under five timing profiles each.
Both tools could be occupied at once and the third worker could use the
first tool returned. Four malformed requests reached the receiver and
produced zero fixture effects. Across the 24 quality cases, each of the six
jobs ran with zero to three rejected inspections. All 24 probes after final
approval were rejected. The case rows, binary hashes and journal digests are
in the [11M results](../../analysis/11m-received-effects.json).

To reproduce the native runs, set `CHOREOFIX_TOOL_CASE_OUTPUT` and
`CHOREOFIX_QUALITY_CASE_OUTPUT` to fresh directories. Run
`cargo +stable test --locked --manifest-path proofs/endpoint-handoff/Cargo.toml --test tool_cabinet -- --test-threads=1`
from the repository root using the recorded dependencies.

The current shared-workstation Wasm results above extend this earlier native
and three-job evidence. Qualification still requires the six jobs' full
quality and rework paths, EPF, actual effects and a completed common score
in the same live run.
